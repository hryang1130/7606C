\begin{abstract}
We study visuomotor Diffusion Policies trained from RGB images on six ManiSkill
manipulation tasks, with all demonstrations generated by motion planning and
all policies evaluated on held-out initial conditions. Under one shared
training configuration we examine three questions. First, the number of
demonstrations needed depends strongly on the task: pushing and pulling
saturate with 100 demonstrations, whereas stacking and placing are still
improving at 400, where they reach 83\% and 96\% success. Second, no denoising
backbone is best everywhere: a Transformer is the most consistent of three, an
MLP falls behind on the longer tasks, and the standard UNet overfits on one
task, reaching 19\% where the Transformer reaches 91\%. Third, fine-tuning on
100 expert corrections collected at the policy's own missed grasps raises
success on one task from 33\% to 47\%, while the same amount of ordinary
demonstrations has no effect. A seventh task, peg insertion, stayed unsolved
in every configuration we tried. We also reproduce one comparison on a Moore
Threads GPU.
\end{abstract}
