\section{Failure Analysis and Limitations}
\label{sec:discussion}
\subsection{Observed failure modes}
The two metrics separate two kinds of failure. Some policies never reach
success: on PlaceSphere, where we classified every evaluation episode by
stage, 374 of the baseline's 600 episodes failed without a stable grasp and
only 29 after one (\cref{sec:takeover}). Others reach success and lose it. On
PushCube the cube enters the goal region and slides out again, so the UNet
succeeds at some point in 95\% of episodes but at the end of only 54\%. On
LiftPegUpright the MLP raises the peg in 41\% of episodes and lets it fall in
most of them (\cref{sec:backbone}).

\textbf{What failures look like.} \Cref{fig:failures} shows one recorded
failure per task. In four of them the policy carries out the whole skill and
ends just outside the success condition. On PickCube the cube is grasped and
lifted but is held beside the goal marker when the episode ends. On StackCube
the red cube is released off-centre on the green one. On PushCube the cube
stops on the rim of the goal region, and on PullCube the gripper moves on to
the target and leaves the cube at its rim. The other two fail during
manipulation: on PlaceSphere the sphere ends on the table beside the bin, and
on LiftPegUpright the peg is grasped, lifted and tilted, and then dropped
flat. These are single episodes chosen for illustration, not a sample; only
PlaceSphere has every failure classified. Other recorded failures of the same
runs are PushCube seeds 10072 and 10092 (the goal region is never reached)
and 10000 (reached and left), PickCube seeds 10002 and 10004, and PullCube
seeds 10025 and 10055.

\textbf{Success that does not last.} How often success is lost depends on how
long the episode runs. The 21 LiftPegUpright data-size checkpoints were
evaluated again with 400 and 800 steps, as separate evaluations on the same
test seeds. At $N=400$, \metriconce{} rises from 79.2\% at 200 steps to
80.6\% and 86.6\%, while \metricend{} falls from 78.4\% to 51.2\% and 53.0\%:
with more time more episodes reach the upright pose, and at 800 steps 39\% of
those no longer hold it at the end. Step logs of failed episodes of one
$N=400$ run show three patterns: the peg stands after release and later tips
over; its pose oscillates around the success threshold; or it is never
grasped, or is held upright above the placing height. The 200-step End column
of \cref{tab:datasize} therefore overstates how often the peg stays upright,
which accounts for the gap between the LiftPegUpright values at $N=100$ in
\cref{tab:datasize} (67.2\%) and \cref{sec:backbone} (49.2\% at 400 steps).

\subsection{Diagnostics and interpretation}
Two diagnostics changed how we read our own numbers. First, the registered
50-step horizon of PlaceSphere is shorter than every demonstration (90--150
steps), and all 16 runs scored zero until they were evaluated with 200 steps:
an evaluation error, not a policy failure. Training now refuses a horizon
shorter than the longest demonstration. Second, denoising loss is not a proxy
for success. On PickCube the UNet has the lowest training loss of the three
backbones and the lowest success; only the validation loss orders the
backbones as success does. Why the UNet overfits on PickCube alone, and
whether the MLP's deficit comes from task difficulty or episode length, remain
hypotheses.

\subsection{Limits of the comparison}
\label{sec:limits}
\textbf{Comparability.} Control mode, horizon, and cameras differ between
tasks, so we compare curve shapes and orderings, not levels. LiftPegUpright
was evaluated with a 200-step horizon in the data-size study and 400 steps in
the backbone study, and some of its demonstrations are longer than both.
\textbf{Statistics.}
Each cell has 3 to 5 training seeds and 100 episodes per seed, and one run
resolves about $\pm8$ points. The StackCube backbone comparison used a z-test,
and the LiftPegUpright and PlaceSphere intervals were computed afterwards from
per-seed rates without pairing episodes. The rule that adds $N=400$ reads the
test results. \textbf{Configuration.} One set of hyperparameters, a fixed 100k
steps, and the final checkpoint are used everywhere, which is what exposes the
UNet's overfitting on PickCube; the backbones also differ in size by a factor
of about 190. \textbf{Scope.} The backbone comparison is missing on
PlaceSphere, the takeover study covers one task and relies on privileged
state, and everything is in simulation with motion-planned demonstrations.
