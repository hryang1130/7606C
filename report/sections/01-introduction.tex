\section{Introduction and Research Questions}
\label{sec:introduction}
Diffusion Policy models action sequences with conditional denoising and
executes them in a receding horizon~\citep{chi2023diffusion}. It is usually
reported with a generous, fixed number of demonstrations and one network
design. Whoever has to collect the demonstrations is left with practical
questions: how many are needed for a given skill, how much the answer depends
on the denoising network, and, once a trained policy fails in a recognizable
way, whether it is better to add more demonstrations or to show the policy how
to recover, as interactive imitation learning suggests~\citep{ross2011dagger}.
We study these questions in ManiSkill~\citep{tao2024maniskill3}, where
demonstrations can be generated at will and policies can be evaluated on many
controlled initial conditions.

\textbf{RQ1 (data efficiency, all six tasks).} How does the number of successful
expert demonstrations affect held-out closed-loop success for RGB Diffusion
Policies, and how does this depend on the task?

\textbf{RQ2 (backbone, five tasks).} What success--efficiency trade-offs are
observed between the implemented UNet, Transformer, and MLP denoisers under the
same experimental protocol?

\textbf{RQ3 (failure correction, one task).} Does fine-tuning on expert takeover
corrections collected at the policy's own failure states improve success beyond
fine-tuning on an equal amount of ordinary demonstrations?

\textbf{Contributions.} We build a pipeline that generates and checks
motion-planned demonstrations and trains a baseline on each of six tasks
(\cref{sec:data,sec:method}); run a data-size study on all six
(\cref{sec:datasize}); compare three denoising backbones on five tasks, with
cost measurements and a reproduction on a Moore Threads GPU
(\cref{sec:backbone,sec:moore}); test expert takeover corrections against a
matched-data control (\cref{sec:takeover}); and diagnose a negative result on
PegInsertionSide (\cref{sec:discussion}).
