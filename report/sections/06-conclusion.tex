\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}
Under one shared configuration, the number of demonstrations an RGB Diffusion
Policy needs for 50\% success ranged from fewer than 25 to about 300 across
six ManiSkill tasks, with learning curves of similar shape shifted along the
data axis (RQ1). The denoising backbone mattered as much as the data on one
task, where a Transformer with 100 demonstrations (90.6\%) beat the UNet with
400 (53.4\%), but no backbone was best on all five tasks compared (RQ2). Where
the policy failed in a recognizable way, 100 expert corrections at its own
missed grasps raised success by 13.8 points while the same amount of ordinary
demonstrations gave nothing, and they taught recovery where more
demonstrations taught fewer misses (RQ3). These findings come from simulation,
motion-planned demonstrations, and few seeds. The natural next experiment is
the backbone comparison on PlaceSphere, to separate task difficulty from
episode length in the MLP's deficit.
